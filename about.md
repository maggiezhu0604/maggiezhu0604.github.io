I am Maggie "Dan" Zhu, an agricultural data scientist and crop science researcher interested in computational breeding for resilient, efficient crop improvement. My work connects plant breeding, quantitative genetics, high-throughput phenotyping, genomic and phenotypic data curation, and decision-support tools for breeding pipelines.

I currently work in pipeline enablement at Bayer Crop Science, where I support experimental protocols and data workflows across breeding, testing, operations, and data science teams. Earlier research experiences in sweet corn phenotyping, edible plant heavy metal contamination, greenhouse systems, and community food safety shaped the way I think about agricultural data: useful models begin with careful biological context, reliable measurements, and communication with the people who will act on the results.

I am preparing for doctoral study in plant breeding and computational breeding. I hope to develop quantitative and genomic tools that make selection more accurate, phenotyping more scalable, and breeding decisions more transparent.

## Research Interests

- Plant breeding, quantitative genetics, and crop improvement
- Genomic selection, genomic prediction, and breeding value estimation
- High-throughput phenotyping and image-based trait extraction
- Breeding pipeline analytics and decision support
- Phenotypic, genomic, and operational data curation
- Food security, sustainability, and data-driven agricultural systems

## Technical Skills

- **Programming and analysis:** R, Python, Excel, image analysis tools
- **Breeding data:** phenotypic data, genomic data, pipeline data curation, experimental protocol review
- **Methods and workflows:** high-throughput phenotyping, data visualization, predictive modeling concepts, breeding analytics
- **Communication:** cross-functional collaboration, technical presentations, workshop facilitation, scientific writing
